# Photo of a plate to ingredients, grams and nutrition: the options

Research for the plated-meal photo path of the recipe analyzer (`/recipes`). It extends [RECIPE_ANALYSIS.md](RECIPE_ANALYSIS.md) and doesn't repeat it: that document covers recipe text, recipe cards and the first round of vendor terms. Links were accessed **2026-11-13** unless noted. Bedrock account setup and models for *recipe* photos are covered separately, so this document doesn't give setup steps.

**How to read the numbers.**
- **UNVERIFIED** means one of three things: the page blocked fetching, only an abstract or secondary source was readable, or the figure is our own estimate.
- **Error metrics differ between papers and can't be compared directly.**
  - *MAPE* is the mean of each dish's percentage error.
  - *MAE ÷ mean*, called "%" by Nutrition5k and "pMAE" elsewhere, usually reads lower than MAPE.
  - *WMAPE* (used by January's benchmark) weights each dish's error by its size.
- **Ground truth varies just as much.** Some sets were weighed: Nutrition5k, ACETADA, OmniFood8K. Others came from app logs, crowd labels or recall standards, and one was partly *generated by Gemini*.
- **Costs are list prices, not measurements.** No model was called for this research.

## The short answer

**No. There is no open-source API you can call that turns a plate photo into ingredients, grams and nutrition, and none worth self-hosting for this app.**

- **The open pieces exist, but as research code.** Google's Nutrition5k dataset is CC BY 4.0 and usable commercially, but ships no model. Food-R1 (Apache-2.0, June 2026) has the best published open result, 14.4% calorie error on Nutrition5k. It was trained partly on research-only data, and in-domain gains like that haven't held up on other people's photos. Everything else is either label-only (Food-101 classifiers), research-only by its data (Meta's inverse cooking, FoodSeg103, UEC), or unlicensed (DietAI24).
- **The APIs that do return grams are commercial, and their terms clash with a diary.** These are SnapCalorie, Passio, LogMeal, FatSecret, Edamam, Calorie Mama and Spike. Several limit what you may store, and several claim rights to the photos users upload.
- **Your guess holds up.** Use a Bedrock vision model to list foods with estimated grams, our existing USDA matcher for the nutrients, and the user to confirm the amounts. This is cheaper than any vendor and cheaper than any GPU.
  - Cost: about **$0.0009 per photo on Claude Haiku 5.5**, or **$0.017 on Sonnet 5.5**.
  - It keeps every nutrient value in our own USDA data.
  - Self-hosting an open model loses on cost below several hundred thousand photos a month, and isn't more accurate zero-shot.
- **What makes the biggest difference to accuracy** is having the user confirm or correct the amounts, not the choice of model. In the studies below, real quantities cut calorie error by about half.
- **Expect this accuracy:**
  - from the photo alone, roughly **20–35% calorie error per meal**;
  - about **14%** when the user supplies real amounts.

## 2. The open-source landscape in more detail

### 2.1 Food-specific research code

- **Nutrition5k** ([repo](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Nutrition5k), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03375)) is the reference benchmark.
  - Cafeteria dishes average 5.7 ingredients each, and every ingredient was weighed as it was added.
  - The paper's baselines (Table 3; % is MAE ÷ mean):

    | Method | Calorie MAE | Mass MAE |
    |---|---|---|
    | Predict the mean | 150.8 kcal (60.2%) | 124.6 g |
    | RGB only | 70.6 kcal (26.1%) | 40.4 g |
    | Depth as a 4th channel | 47.6 kcal (18.8%) | — |
    | Depth-derived volume | 41.3 kcal (16.5%) | 29.4 g (13.7%) |

  - **People estimating mass from the same images were off by 53% (non-experts) and 41% (nutritionists)**, on 10 plates.
  - No weights are released.
  - Ingredient nutrients come from USDA FNDDS 4.1, but the 554-row `ingredients_metadata.csv` has names and per-gram macros only.
  - A 2026 human review found its labels systematically **omit** components ([Sterling et al., bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.26.740845v1), abstract only).
- **Data licenses are the trap.**
  - Recipe1M is research-only (wording UNVERIFIED), and it underlies inverse cooking, FoodSeg103's images, FoodLMM and part of Food-R1.
  - UEC-Food and UECFoodPix are "non-commercial research purpose" only.
  - Food-101 images allow only "scientific fair use".
  - For a paid product, avoid weights trained on these, or get legal advice. This is a provenance risk, not a settled legal question.
- **Portion and volume research** reaches about 10% volume error, but only with multi-view capture or depth.
  - The CVPR 2024 MetaFood winner, VolETA, scored 10.97% MAPE with a checkerboard and many frames, and about 19% from a single image. Its code is **GPL-3.0**.
  - MetaFood3D is useful because each item carries an 8-digit **FNDDS 5.0 code**, but its license is UNVERIFIED.
  - None of this fits a "one upload" web flow.
- **End-to-end open "photo to nutrition" projects on GitHub** are student projects (Food-101 or YOLO plus a calorie table) or thin wrappers around GPT or Gemini. **None found that is commercially licensed and maintained.**

**What this means for us.**
1. **Nobody has published Claude 5.x or Amazon Nova results on food photos.** The closest are Claude Sonnet 4.5 on DiningBench and the Claude 4.5/4.6 study.
2. **Model tier matters on photos.** Haiku 4.5 was clearly worse than Sonnet and Opus 4.6. Don't assume Haiku 5.5 is good enough for plates without testing it.
3. **Our "list, then look up USDA" design is supported, with a caveat.** It helps most with grams, macros and auditability; on calories it is roughly even with direct estimation. Its real advantage is that every nutrient comes from USDA and can be edited.

**Caveats on these prices.**
- **Claude 5.5 in us-east-1 runs only through US-geo or global inference profiles**, with no in-region option. Global is 10% cheaper ($0.10 / $0.50 for Haiku) but may process requests outside the US.
- **Bedrock has no batch pricing for Claude 5.5.** Nova and most open models get 50% off in batch. Batch doesn't suit an interactive upload anyway.
- **Output tokens cost as much as the image does.** Keep the JSON schema tight. Shrinking the photo to about 1000×750 (972 tokens) cuts input cost by about a third. Whether that costs accuracy is untested.
- **AWS publishes no food or nutrition accuracy for Nova.** The only AWS sample we found ([serverless-genai-food-analyzer-app](https://github.com/aws-samples/serverless-genai-food-analyzer-app)) lists fridge ingredients for recipes and gets calories from Open Food Facts barcodes. It reports no accuracy.

CMUs per model are only known after import (UNVERIFIED) |
| **Lambda (CPU only)**: 10,240 MB max, about 6 vCPU, no GPU ([quotas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lambda/latest/dg/gettingstarted-limits.html)) | A 2B model, e.g. Qwen3-VL-2B | 10 GB × ~60 s ≈ $0.008 per photo (our estimate) | Pay per use | ~$0.008, **about 9× Haiku 5.5**, with 30–90 s latency (UNVERIFIED estimate) and a model with no food benchmark |

**Verdict: self-hosting doesn't win at this app's scale.**
- **Cost.** One always-on L4 costs as much as about **680,000 Haiku 5.5 photos** or about **34,000 Sonnet 5.5 photos** a month.
  - SageMaker can scale endpoints to zero ([since Nov 2024](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/11/amazon-sagemaker-scale-down-zero-ai-inference-save-costs/)), but a GPU cold start takes minutes. That is unacceptable for someone waiting on a photo (the exact time is UNVERIFIED).
  - We'd also own patching, scaling and the GPU quota requests.
- **Accuracy.** Zero-shot open models are about level with Claude Sonnet 4.5 on DiningBench and behind GPT-4.1 and Claude 3.7 on weighed meals. Only a fine-tuned model (Food-R1) beats frontier models, and only in-domain, with data-provenance and transfer risk.
- **When to revisit.**
  - Photo volume passes about 30–50k a month on a Sonnet-class model.
  - Or a commercially clean fine-tune beats Bedrock on **our** evaluation set, not just on Nutrition5k.
- **If the owner specifically wants open weights**, Bedrock already serves Qwen3-VL-235B, Gemma 3 and Llama 4 per token with no GPU to run. Put them in the evaluation alongside Claude and Nova; switching later is a model-ID change.

## 6. Portion-size aids: what has evidence

| Aid | Evidence | Strength for a web app |
|---|---|---|
| **User confirms or enters amounts** (grams, or household measures we convert) | **Strong and directly relevant to VLMs.** GPT-5 energy MAPE fell from 30.5% to 13.9% given ingredients and amounts ([Rodríguez-Jiménez 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/)). Gemini 2.5 Flash carb MAPE fell from 56.6% to 20.2% given the true weight ([Mu et al. 2025](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/computerscience_fac_pubs/417)). Nutrition5k: a model that knows the mass gets 9.5% vs 26.1%. **But unaided guesses by people are poor**: 41–53% mass error in Nutrition5k's human study. ASA24 portion *images* got only 13% of reports within 10% of true intake, vs 31% for text household measures, and overestimated liquids by 118% ([Lucassen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9291996/)) | **Do this.** Pre-fill the model's grams, show household equivalents ("about 1 cup"), make editing one tap, and ask about the things photos miss (oil, butter, dressing, sugar in drinks). Present amounts in household text, not a photo atlas |
| **Reference object in frame** (card, coin, fiducial marker) | Established for dietitian- and algorithm-scored systems: mFR/TADA with a fiducial marker had +1.3% energy bias in a 152-person feeding study ([Whitton 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11347807)), but there was no arm without a marker. For VLMs: one weak single-author preprint, GPT-4o 18.7% with a reference vs 28.9% without, on different image sets ([Xu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04491)) | **Optional experiment.** Cheap to offer ("put a fork or a card beside the plate"); test it in our evaluation before promoting it |
| **Plate or bowl size** | Classical methods using the plate as a reference got 12% volume RMSE ([Jia 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3328298)). No VLM study found | **Low-cost experiment:** a "small / dinner / large plate" selector passed to the model as context |
| **Second photo at about 45°, or a short video** | Multi-view 3D reconstruction is about twice as accurate as single view (MetaFood: about 7–9% vs 19% volume MAPE; [Dehais 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03330) 8–10%). Not tested with VLMs; a second image also doubles the image-token cost | **Not now.** The evidence is for 3D pipelines we wouldn't build |
| **Depth (phone LiDAR or structured light)** | Nutrition5k: calories 26.1% to 16.5% with depth-derived volume. iPhone structured light gave 12.7% energy error ([Herzig 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7142738/)). No peer-reviewed iPhone LiDAR food study found | **Not possible from a web page**: browsers don't expose LiDAR depth. Only relevant if a native app is built |
| **Extra context** (time, place, meal type) | Only 1.5–6 points of improvement for frontier models on ACETADA | Minor |

## 7. Recommended architecture

This is the current design ([RECIPES.md](../RECIPES.md)) with a plate-specific prompt and a stronger confirmation step. **No new infrastructure.**

```
plate photo (browser shrinks it to 1568 px and strips EXIF, as now)
  + optional context: meal type, plate size, "anything not visible?" (oil, butter, dressing, sugar)
   │
   ▼  Bedrock vision model, one call, strict JSON schema
items: name, preparation (fried, grilled…), estimated grams, household measure,
       visible | assumed (e.g. cooking oil), confidence
   │
   ▼  lib/recipes/match.ts → USDA (Foundation, SR Legacy, FNDDS mixed dishes)
   │  The model never supplies nutrient values
   ▼
"Check the amounts" step: every row pre-filled and editable; assumed items shown separately;
   totals shown as a range until amounts are confirmed
   │
   ▼  lib/recipes/nutrition.ts → totals from USDA values; saved as now
```

**Choices and reasons.**

1. **Which model, before an evaluation.**
   - Start the photo path on **Claude Sonnet 5.5 (about $0.017 per photo)**. Keep Haiku 5.5 ($0.0009) for text.
   - Reason: Haiku-tier Claude was clearly worse on food photos in the only study that compared tiers.
   - At 1,000 photos a month the Sonnet cost is about $17. If the evaluation shows Haiku 5.5 is close enough, switch and save about 95%.
   - The app currently defaults to Opus 5.5 at about $0.035, which is unlikely to be worth twice Sonnet's price; the 4.6 study found Opus and Sonnet within a few points.
2. **Run a small evaluation before charging for it.** This was already recommended in RECIPE_ANALYSIS.md. For photos:
   - **Data:**
     - about 50 home-weighed meals of our own (the only data that matches our users);
     - a few hundred Nutrition5k dishes (CC BY 4.0, weighed, USDA-based; fetch selected images, not the 181 GB archive);
     - optionally the January benchmark's 1,000 phone photos (CC BY 4.0, not weighed).
   - **Models:** Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Nova 2 Lite, and one open-weight Bedrock model (Qwen3-VL-235B or Gemma 3 27B).
   - **Measures:** item recall, gram error, and calorie error after USDA matching, with and without simulated user correction. Also test the reference-object and plate-size prompts.
3. **Make the prompt plate-specific.**
   - Ask for each item's *preparation*, since "fried" vs "grilled" changes the USDA match.
   - Ask for *assumed hidden ingredients* as separate rows. Open-KNEAD's recipe priors for hidden cooking fat were where its calorie gains came from.
   - Tell the model to ignore text in the image. This defends against prompt injection and helped stronger models in the Claude 4.6 study.
4. **Use FNDDS mixed dishes for composite foods.**
   - The database already holds 1,598 FNDDS mixed dishes, and both DietAI24 and Open-KNEAD ground to FNDDS. A "chicken burrito" often matches better as one FNDDS dish than as six guessed components.
   - Let the model offer either form, and let the user choose.
   - Adding more FNDDS categories (`--fndds-all`, see [KEY_NUTRIENTS.md](../KEY_NUTRIENTS.md)) is a separate decision worth testing in the evaluation.
5. **Portion aids, in order:**
   - (a) a confirmation step with household measures (do it);
   - (b) an optional plate-size selector and an optional "add a fork or card for scale" tip (evaluate);
   - (c) skip multi-photo and depth on the web.
6. **Don't use** a commercial photo API (storage terms, data rights, $100–500 a month minimums) or a self-hosted GPU (see §5).

**Cost per photo.** About **$0.017 on Sonnet 5.5 or $0.0009 on Haiku 5.5**, plus about $0.0001 of Lambda time. The optional re-rank call is extra. At 1,000 photos a month that's $17 or $0.90.

**Expected accuracy.** This is inferred from the studies above; nobody has measured Claude 5.5.
- **Photo only, before the user edits anything:** about **20–35% calorie error per meal**, worse for large portions and for dishes with hidden fat. Means can look better than individual meals because over- and underestimates cancel.
- **After the user confirms real amounts** (weighed, from a package, or from a recipe): about **10–15%**. The remaining error comes from matching and from cooking that USDA values don't capture.
- **Micronutrients:** better than a model guessing them directly, because the composition comes from USDA. Portion error still carries over, and sodium and fat depend on hidden ingredients the photo can't show.

## 8. What to tell users

Keep the current "rough estimate" labelling and ±35% calorie range for unconfirmed photo results. Suggested wording:

> **A photo gives a starting point, not a measurement.** We recognise the foods well, but amounts are hard to judge from a picture. In one study even nutritionists misjudged portion weights from photos by about 40%. Calories may be 20–35% off until you check the amounts. Adjust them below; if you know the weight or the package size, use it. Oil, butter, dressings and sugar often don't show in a photo, so add them if they were used. Nutrient values come from USDA FoodData Central.

Also:
- After the user edits amounts, narrow the displayed range.
- Never show a photo-derived result as precise to the calorie.
- Don't use photo-derived micronutrients, especially sodium, vitamin D and folate, for anything health-critical.
- Keep the existing note that photos aren't stored.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Accuracy per meal is wide** even when the average looks good; large portions are underestimated ([Fridolfsson 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/)) | Ranges, the confirmation step, honest copy. No medical or clinical claims |
| **No published Claude 5.x or Nova food results**, so model choice is a guess | Run the §7 evaluation before charging; log edit distances (how much users change grams) as an ongoing quality signal |
| **Cuisine and camera bias**: Nutrition5k is California cafeterias, DiningBench is Chinese restaurants; fine-tunes don't transfer | Use our own weighed set; collect consented, opt-in corrections later |
| **Licenses**: Recipe1M-, Food-101- and UEC-derived weights; Qwen2.5-VL-3B is research-only; Depth Anything V2 Base/Large is non-commercial; DietAI24 has no license; the Llama multimodal EU exclusion; VolETA is GPL-3.0 | Stay on Bedrock models, or Apache-2.0 weights with known data. Re-check any license before shipping it |
| **Vendor data rights and storage limits** (LogMeal, Passio, Azumio and SnapCalorie can use uploaded data; FatSecret 24 h; Edamam only while subscribed) | Not using a vendor avoids this. If one is added, store only its labels and grams and recompute nutrients from USDA |
| **Cost drift**: the Opus default is about 2× Sonnet and 40× Haiku; output tokens dominate | Set `AI_MODEL` explicitly for photos; tight schema; the existing daily budget cap and planned DynamoDB quotas |
| **Prompt injection through text in the photo** | Instruct the model to ignore text in the image; the schema-only output and the USDA-only nutrients already limit the damage |
| **Data residency**: global inference profiles may process outside the US | Use the US-geo profile (10% more) if that matters |
| **Benchmark contamination**: public sets like Nutrition5k may be in model training data | Weight our own weighed meals most heavily |
