# Nutrition Database: Sources, Schema and Nutrient Definitions

`food-nutrition-app/nutrition.db` is generated by `scripts/build_db.py` from USDA
FoodData Central (FDC) bulk downloads. Don't edit it by hand. Rebuild it and then
validate it (see [VERIFICATION_INSTRUCTIONS.md](VERIFICATION_INSTRUCTIONS.md)).

The machine-readable version of this page is `scripts/nutrient_spec.py`, which
both the builder and the validator use.

## Sources

| Dataset | Release | Format used | Included | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation Foods | 2026-10-29 | JSON | 399 of 424 foods | Newest analytical data. The CSV zip for this release is incomplete (it has no `food.csv`, `food_nutrient.csv` or `food_portion.csv`), so the builder reads the JSON. JSON values are rounded to 3 significant digits, as on the FDC website. |
| SR Legacy | 2018-04 (final) | CSV | 7,448 of 7,793 | Covers most everyday foods with near-complete nutrient panels. Excludes *Baby Foods*. |
| FNDDS / Survey foods | 2024-10-31 (FNDDS 2021-2023) | CSV | 1,598 of 5,432 | Only the WWEIA categories 3002-3808 are included: mixed dishes, pizza, sandwiches, burgers, burritos/tacos, stir-fries and soups. |

Download page: <https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/>. The zip URLs
(`https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/fdc-datasets/<file>`) and their sha256 hashes are
pinned in `SOURCES` in `build_db.py`. They are also recorded in the `meta`
table, under key `sources`. FDC data is public domain (CC0).

**Why FNDDS is included only partly.** FNDDS is the only USDA dataset with
full, consistent nutrient profiles for composite meals such as lasagna,
burritos, pad thai or chicken soup. SR Legacy has few of these. The other
FNDDS categories are left out for two reasons:

- About 3,700 of them restate single foods that SR Legacy or Foundation
  already cover ("Apple, raw", "Milk, whole").
- They add many near-duplicate variants. For example, there are 109 coffee
  and 45 tea entries.

`--fndds-all` adds them back. Baby foods, infant formula and human milk
(WWEIA 9002-9602) are always excluded unless you pass `--include-baby-foods`.

**Excluded Foundation foods.** 25 Foundation foods report no energy and only
part of the protein/fat/carbohydrate panel, and replace no SR Legacy row that
has energy, so their calories can't be determined. Most are the "Beans, Dry,
... (0% moisture)" research samples. These foods are listed in
`meta.excluded_foods_no_energy`. (Ten others in the same state, such as
rutabaga, shallots, tomatillos and prune juice, take their energy from the SR
Legacy row they replace: rule 7 below.)

## Handling foods that appear in more than one dataset

- Every row is kept, and `data_type` is preserved (`foundation_food`,
  `sr_legacy_food`, `survey_fndds_food`). `data_type_rank` is 1, 2 or 3 in
  that order. Sort search results by `data_type_rank`, then by relevance.
- 261 SR Legacy rows describe the same food as a Foundation food, analysed
  earlier. These rows have `superseded_by_fdc_id` set to the Foundation
  `fdc_id` (`meta.superseded_link_counts` says how each was linked):
  - **211 share an NDB number** with the Foundation food. USDA kept the
    number when it re-analysed these foods. USDA also reused a number for a
    different food at least once (Foundation "Apples, honeycrisp, with skin,
    raw" has golden delicious's number), so the NDB links listed in
    [`scripts/data/foundation_sr_ndb_unlinked.csv`](../scripts/data/foundation_sr_ndb_unlinked.csv)
    are not made: both foods show, and neither fills the other's gaps.
  - **50 are linked by a reviewed crosswalk**,
    [`scripts/data/foundation_sr_crosswalk.csv`](../scripts/data/foundation_sr_crosswalk.csv).
    USDA gave these Foundation foods a new NDB number (100xxx), so the NDB
    match can't find their SR twin (for example "Fish, cod, Atlantic, wild
    caught, raw" and "Fish, cod, Atlantic, raw"). Each row names both foods
    and says why they are the same item.
- A pair goes in the crosswalk only when it is **the exact same item**: same
  commodity, species and variety, same part or cut and trim, same state (raw,
  cooked and how, canned, dried, puffed), same fat level, salt and
  enrichment. Labelling differences don't count against a pair: "wild caught"
  or "farm raised", "frozen" vs "(may have been previously frozen)",
  "organic", a brand name for the same food (KAMUT khorasan), where the samples
  came from ("restaurant"), "from concentrate" for single-strength juice,
  "raw" vs SR's "dried" for shelled nuts, and edible-portion wording that is
  SR's edible portion anyway ("peeled" rutabaga, "dehusked" tomatillos). A
  variety or colour named on one side only is accepted only when the SR entry
  plainly is that variety (SR raw asparagus is green; SR's plain radish is the
  red one). An SR row maps to at most one Foundation food and is never also
  linked by NDB number. The 135 near-misses that were rejected, each with the
  reason, are in
  [`foundation_sr_crosswalk_rejected.csv`](../scripts/data/foundation_sr_crosswalk_rejected.csv)
  (for example different beef trim, onion colour, potatoes with or without
  skin, blue swimming crab vs blue crab). The crosswalk is tied to the pinned
  Foundation release: a pair that names a food the release doesn't have stops
  the build, and `verify_data_integrity.py` checks every pair against the
  database.
- Superseded rows are kept because Foundation rows often have gaps (only 20%
  of Foundation foods report vitamin D, compared with 66% of SR Legacy
  foods). The site hides them from search, categories, rankings and the
  sitemap, their page points to the Foundation food, and the Foundation food's
  missing values are filled from them at run time and marked † (see
  `lib/nutrient-fill.ts`). A value neither entry has stays NULL.
- Values are never merged across datasets in the database. The one exception
  is the calories fallback described below, and it is recorded.
- `fdc_id` is the stable key across rebuilds. `foods.id` is reassigned on
  every build, in the order data_type_rank, description, fdc_id.

## Schema (schema_version 2)

`foods` keeps every v1 column, in the same order and with the same meaning.
New columns are added after them.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id`, `fdc_id`, `description`, `data_type`, `food_category`, `brand_owner`, `serving_size` (=100), `serving_size_unit` (='g') | v1. `food_category` holds the FDC food category for Foundation and SR Legacy foods, and the WWEIA category for FNDDS foods. `brand_owner` is always NULL. |
| nutrient columns (table below) | Amount per **100 g edible portion**. **NULL = not reported**, 0 = reported as zero. |
| `calories_source` | Which energy rule produced `calories` (see below). |
| `data_type_rank` | 1 = Foundation, 2 = SR Legacy, 3 = FNDDS. |
| `ndb_number`, `fndds_food_code` | Source identifiers. |
| `superseded_by_fdc_id` | Set on SR Legacy rows that a Foundation food replaces: same NDB number (unless the pair is in `scripts/data/foundation_sr_ndb_unlinked.csv`), or a pair in `scripts/data/foundation_sr_crosswalk.csv`. |
| `publication_date`, `source_release` | Provenance. |

Other tables:

- `serving_sizes(id, food_id, label, weight_g)` has the same schema as v1 and
  now has an index on `food_id`. Every food has a `"100 g"` row (100 g). The
  other rows come from `food_portion` in the source dataset. Labels look like
  `1 cup, chopped`, `1 RACC`, `3 oz` or `1 enchilada, any size`. Zero-weight
  rows and duplicates are dropped. FNDDS's "Quantity not specified" portion
  is labelled `Quantity not specified (typical portion)`.
- `foods_fts` is an FTS5 table with external content on `foods`
  (`content_rowid = foods.id`). It indexes `description` and `food_category`,
  uses the `unicode61 remove_diacritics 2` tokenizer and prefix indexes for 2
  and 3 characters. Triggers keep it in sync. Example query:
  `SELECT f.* FROM foods_fts JOIN foods f ON f.id = foods_fts.rowid WHERE foods_fts MATCH 'chick* breast' ORDER BY f.data_type_rank, bm25(foods_fts)`.
- `meta(key, value)` holds provenance:
  - `schema_version`, `builder_version`, `build_date_utc`
  - `sources` (JSON: name, release, URL, sha256)
  - `count_<data_type>`, `foods_count`, `serving_sizes_count`
  - `calories_source_counts`, `nutrient_coverage_pct`, `excluded_foods_no_energy`
  - `superseded_link_counts` (`ndb_number`, `crosswalk`) and
    `foundation_sr_crosswalk` and `foundation_sr_ndb_unlinked` (file, sha256
    and number of pairs used)
  - `duplicate_policy` and other notes
- `value_provenance(food_id, nutrient_column, source)` has one row for each
  value that did not come from the column's primary nutrient id, such as
  `fdc_1063`, `rae_from_components` or `clamped_negative:-0.43`.

- Added sugars (1235): no included dataset reports it. FNDDS added sugars

- Vitamin D or A in IU: these duplicate the µg columns.

Coverage percentages are for the 2026-11 build. They are recomputed into
`meta.nutrient_coverage_pct` on every build. Foundation rows are much sparser
than SR Legacy rows. For example, Foundation reports vitamin A for 21% of
foods and folate for 42%.

## Energy (`calories`, `calories_source`)

1. `fdc_1008_energy`: Energy, kcal (9,141 foods: all SR Legacy and FNDDS,
   and some Foundation)
2. `fdc_2048_atwater_specific`: Foundation, Atwater specific factors (200)
3. `fdc_2047_atwater_general`: Foundation, Atwater general factors (79)
4. `fdc_1062_energy_kj`: kJ ÷ 4.184 (currently unused)
5. `computed_atwater_4_4_9`: 4 × protein + 4 × carbohydrate + 9 × fat
   (+ 7 × alcohol). Used **only** when protein, fat and carbohydrate are all
   reported (currently unused).
6. `sr_legacy_ndb_match_1008`: Foundation foods only. Uses the energy (1008)
   of the SR Legacy food with the same NDB number (15 foods, e.g. butter,
   oils, raisins, prunes, salt).
7. `sr_legacy_crosswalk_1008`: Foundation foods only. Uses the energy (1008)
   of the SR Legacy food the crosswalk links it to, the first one listed if
   there are several (10 foods: rutabaga, shallots, tomatillos, green onion,
   spaghetti squash, rhubarb, mayonnaise, and prune, tart cherry and
   pomegranate juice).

If none of these apply, the food is excluded.

In the old database, 224 of 411 foods had every nutrient stored as 0
(calories, protein, fat, carbohydrate and all micronutrients). The cause was
that energy for those foods exists only as 2047/2048, and the ingestion code
fell back to SR Legacy only through the API. Raisins, for example, would have
been computed as 0 kcal under a naive "macros with missing = 0" rule. That
is why rule 5 requires all three macros.

## Known source anomalies (kept as published)

- SR 173174 "SLIMFAST Shake Mix ... whey powder": manganese 269 mg/100 g.
  This is a likely USDA error.
- 9 foods where SFA + MUFA + PUFA exceeds total fat by more than 5%, for
  example Foundation "Bread, white, commercial", "Quinoa, puffed" and some SR
  beef cuts. SR MUFA/PUFA totals for hydrogenated fats also include trans
  isomers.
- 2 foods where sugars exceed carbohydrate: Foundation 749420 "Pork, cured,
  bacon, cooked, restaurant" and SR 171627 "Ham, chopped".
- Energy differs from 4/4/9 for sugar-free/polyol products, vinegars
  (acetic acid), leavening agents, oat bran and alkalized cocoa (SR specific
  factors). These are whitelisted in the validator.
